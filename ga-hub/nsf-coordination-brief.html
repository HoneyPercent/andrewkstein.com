    NSF 26-508 · AI-Ready America
    Georgia's AI Coordination Hub bid
    A working-group briefing: the grant, the timeline, and where our group can help.
    Georgia AI working group June 24, 2026

    1
    Just the facts

    NSF TechAccess: AI-Ready America

    A national program funding one AI Coordination Hub per state — a backbone that connects a state's existing AI efforts across education, workforce, industry, and government.
    The figures
    Program
    NSF 26-508, Program Area 1 — State / Territory Coordination Hubs
    Award
    $1M per year for 3 years per hub — a 4th year is possible (~$168–224M across all hubs)
    Scope
    Up to 56 hubs — one per state, DC, or territory
    Rounds
    3 rounds — 10 funded in Round 1, 20 in Round 2, the rest in Round 3
    Georgia
    In Round 1 — Georgia Tech filed the Letter of Intent; full proposal due July 16, 2026
    To apply
    A 15-page plan (5 sections) — no product required; cost-sharing is prohibited
    The hub's 5 responsibilities — what it does
    R1 · AI Learning & Resource NavigatorMaintain a public inventory of the state's AI resources
    R2 · AI Readiness Strategic PlanA statewide roadmap with data collection + evaluation
    R3 · AI Deployment SupportHands-on advisory + technical help for orgs adopting AI
    R4 · Training & Capacity BuildingCoordinate workforce partners; align credentials
    R5 · Priority-Sector CoordinationConvene agriculture, healthcare, manufacturing, energy
    Source: NSF 26-508 solicitation

    A model that worked

    What Mississippi built

    Mississippi ran the first state AI network and is the most-cited model. Read closely, it can point to what the program rewards.
    How it's often described
    A catalog of AI courses for residents
    • Read as a library of curated training content
    • The headline most people remember
    • Easy to picture — and easy to misread
    →
    How it describes itself
    “One coordinated network”
    • 23 institutions, agencies, and sector pilots
    • Aligned under shared goals and measurement
    • The courses sit inside the network, not the other way around
    What earned the award was the coordination — the connective tissue across a state's existing efforts.
    Source: Mississippi AI Network (mainms.org)

    What reviewers score

    Proposal scoring

    Five merit criteria, one per section of the 15-page proposal.
    (a)
    Vision & approach
    A credible plan to advance statewide AI readiness
    §1
    (b)
    Team & partners
    Statewide convening capacity, plus the expertise to run it
    §2
    (c)
    Current state & gaps
    A real read of what exists in Georgia and where the holes are
    §3
    (d)
    Work plan & metrics
    Realistic milestones and measurable outcomes
    §4
    (e)
    Resource mobilization
    Leverage beyond the NSF money (cost-sharing is not allowed)
    §5
    No criteria mention a content library. Reviewers are scoring the plan to build the hub — not proof a hub already exists. Content still has a home: a resource inventory (R1) or training (R4) is work the funded hub can do — a later phase, not a prerequisite.
    Distinct from the hub's five responsibilities (slide 2): responsibilities are what the hub does; criteria are how the proposal is scored.
    Source: NSF 26-508 merit-review criteria
