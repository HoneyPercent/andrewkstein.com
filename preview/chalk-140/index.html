7 Design Changes in This Preview

This redesign applies newsletter best practices while staying within CIE's existing brand palette and Mailchimp workflow. Each change is independent — adopt any combination.

  1. Clean masthead — CIE logo, prominent title, issue number, date. No competing CTAs in the header. Ken's editorial hook sets the tone.
  2. Content type badges — FREE / CIE+ labels tell readers instantly what's open vs. premium.
  3. Card-based articles with imagery — Each piece gets its own scannable card with headline, thumbnail, summary, and "Read more."
  4. Visual hierarchy — The lead story gets hero treatment; "In this issue" summary and reading time up top. Supporting articles are smaller cards.
  5. Separated CTAs — Membership CTA inside the CIE+ section where interest peaks; donation in its own warm-colored block — not buried in text.
  6. Typography & texture — Merriweather (serif) for headlines signals scholarship; the original uses Helvetica uniformly. Subtle textures, gradient dividers, and micro-interactions add premium feel.
  7. Event calendar cards — Dates, speaker photos, and details at a glance with structured layout instead of inline paragraphs.
1 Clean Masthead

The original header crams logo, donate button, membership CTA, and Ken's title together. This version uses the CIE logo subtly, lets the brand breathe, and adds Ken's one-sentence editorial hook for personal connection. CTAs move to dedicated sections below where they perform better.

3 Cards with Imagery

Instead of article text flowing continuously with thin dividers, each piece gets its own bordered card with a thumbnail image. The Turkey and Iran articles retain their visuals. Readers can scan headlines and choose what to click — proven to increase engagement.

5 Separated CTAs

The original buries a donation ask in a text paragraph. This version gives membership and donation each a distinct, warm-colored block with clear pricing and action button. The CIE+ membership CTA lives inside the CIE+ section where interest is highest.

6 Typography & Texture

The original uses Helvetica at uniform sizes — everything looks the same. This version uses Merriweather (serif) for headlines, signaling scholarly authority. IBM Plex Sans for body text adds institutional character. Subtle noise textures, gradient dividers, and hover micro-interactions create a premium editorial feel without being flashy.

2 Content Type Badges

Every article is tagged FREE or CIE+. Readers instantly see what's open to everyone vs. what's a membership perk. An EDITOR'S PICK badge highlights the must-read article — social proof that guides scanning behavior.

4 Visual Hierarchy

The lead story gets a gradient hero banner with the article's own image behind it — it's the one thing you want every subscriber to see. An "In this issue" summary lets readers decide in 10 seconds whether to keep reading. A reading time estimate ("~3 min read") reduces the psychological barrier to engaging.

7 Event Calendar Cards

The original lists events in prose paragraphs. This version uses structured date blocks with speaker photos — readers can scan for "is this near me, who's speaking, and when?" in under 2 seconds.
